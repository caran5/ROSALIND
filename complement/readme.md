- The search for a compelte chemical structure of nucleic acids was central to the molecular biolgy research in the mid-2oth centrury -  culminating in 1053 with a publication in Nature of fewer than 800 words by Watson and Crick
- Watson and Crick proposed the following structure for DNA:
1. The DNA molecule is made up of two strands, running in opposite directions
2. Each base bonds to a base in the opposite strand. Adenine always bonds with thymine, cystosine with guanine - the complement of a abse is the base to which it always bond.
3. The two strands are twisted together into a long spiral staircase structure called a double helix. 
- Because they dictate how bases from different strands interact with each other, (1) and (2) above compoase the secondary structure of DNA. (3) describes the 3-D shape of the DNA molecule or its tertiary structure.
- In light of Watson-Crick's model, the bonding of two complementary bases is called a base pair; therefore, the length of a DNA molecule will commobly be given in bp instead of nt. By complementarity, once we know the order of bases on one strand, we can immediately deduce the sequence of bases in the complementary strand. These bases will run in the opposite order to match the fact that two strands run in opposite directions.

In DNA strings, A and T are the complements of each other as well as C and G. 

The reverse complement of a DNA string s is the string s^c formed by reversing the symbols of s, then taking the complement of each symbol; such as GTCA is TGAC. 

Given a DNA string s of length at most 1000 bp, return the reverse compliment of s^c of s.
